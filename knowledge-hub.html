Clay Soil – Detailed Rules

  • East Midlands clay is fertile but heavy, cold in spring, bakes and cracks in summer, holds winter water.
  • 1. Never work it wet. If it sticks to boots and tools, leave it.
  • 2. Feed from the top with bulky organic matter (well-rotted compost or manure). One barrowload per square metre makes a real difference.
  • 3. Do not add sand to heavy clay – it can set like concrete.
  • 4. Raised beds and no-dig improve drainage and stop trampling.
  • 5. Mulch permanent plants (rhubarb, fruit, roses) to stop summer cracking.
  • Advantages: naturally fertile, holds water and nutrients well once structure is improved. Excellent for brassicas and beans.

No-Dig Method – Detailed

  • No routine digging or rotavating. Cover the ground each year with a thick layer of well-rotted organic matter (minimum 5 cm / 2 in, better 8–15 cm when starting). Worms and fungi do the work.
  • Starting a new bed: remove perennial weeds first, then overlapping cardboard (turf can stay under it), then 10–15 cm of compost. Plant straight in.
  • Yearly maintenance: lighter top-up mulch (5 cm is about 50 litres per square metre).
  • Paths: woodchip or cardboard-plus-woodchip keeps boots clean. Keep beds about 4 ft (1.2 m) wide so you can reach the middle without treading.
  • Lasagna beds work on the same principle but watch for slug hotels on the sides.

Climate, Frost And Timing (Nottingham / Ruddington Area)

  • Last spring air frost: typically mid-April (city) to late April / early May in outlying or low-lying spots. Practical plant-out date for tender crops is end of May (RHS Midlands guidance).
  • First autumn air frost: typically early-to-mid November (can be mid-October some years).
  • Ground frost continues 2–4 weeks after the last air frost.
  • Hardy crops (broad beans, peas, brassicas, onions, potatoes): mid-April onwards, with fleece ready.
  • Tender crops (tomatoes, courgettes, beans, squash): late May in a normal year.
  • Always check the current forecast before planting out.

Monthly Calendar – Detailed Jobs

  • January: Plan rotation and beds. Order seeds. Chit first-early potatoes late in the month. Clean tools.
  • February: Sow indoors (broad beans, peas, onions, leeks, lettuce, early brassicas). Top up no-dig beds. Do not walk on wet clay.
  • March: Sow outdoors under cover (carrots, parsnips, peas, spinach, radish). Plant first-early potatoes late March. Sow tomatoes and peppers indoors.
  • April: Main outdoor sowing month. Plant second-early potatoes. Harden off tender seedlings but do not plant out yet. Fleece ready for late snaps.
  • May: Plant out tender crops late in the month. Sow successional salads and French beans. Net brassicas against pigeons. Hoe little and often.
  • June: Finish planting tender crops. First harvests (broad beans, early potatoes). Watch blackfly and blight risk.
  • July: Harvest potatoes, beans, garlic, early courgettes. Sow spring cabbage and swede. Water fruiting crops. Blight watch.
  • August: Main harvest. Sow overwintering salads and spring cabbage. Clear finished beds and sow green manures or mulch.
  • September: Plant out spring cabbage. Harvest maincrop potatoes as foliage dies – do not leave them in wet clay. Cure squash.
  • October: Plant garlic and overwintering onion sets. Sow overwintering broad beans (Aquadulce). Mulch no-dig beds. Lift and store potatoes.
  • November: First frosts. Last garlic plantings if ground workable. Mulch beds and rhubarb. Check stores.
  • December: Quiet month. Plan, order seeds, clean tools, check stores. Mulch parsnips.

Crop Guides

  • Potatoes: Chit Feb/Mar. Early varieties from mid-late March, maincrop April. Earth up or mulch. Lift as foliage dies rather than leaving in wet clay. Slug-resistant varieties: Charlotte, Kestrel, Estima, Pentland Dell.
  • Brassicas: Net against pigeons and cabbage whites. Lime only if soil test shows acid. Grow on in pots before planting. Clubroot-resistant options: Crispus F1 sprouts, Clapton F1 cauliflower, Kilaxy/Kilaton cabbages.
  • Tomatoes: Sow indoors March. Plant out after late May. Under cover is more reliable. Water at roots. Look for blight-resistant outdoor varieties.
  • Beans & Peas: Peas from March. Runner and French beans after mid-May. Pinch broad bean tips once pods set to reduce blackfly.
  • Onions, Garlic, Leeks: Garlic Oct–Nov. Onion sets March/April. Leeks sown spring, planted summer.
  • Courgettes: One or two plants usually enough. Plenty of compost and sun.
  • Salads: Succession sow from spring. Winter salads under cloches.

Pests And Problems (Organic-First)

  • Slugs: Worst on clay and wet autumns. Torchlight picking, traps, encourage predators. Lift potatoes promptly. Slug-resistant potato varieties help. Nematodes work less well on poorly drained clay.
  • Aphids / Blackfly: Squash colonies. Pinch broad bean tips. Encourage ladybirds.
  • Potato & Tomato Blight: Warm wet weather. No sprays for UK gardeners. Wider spacing, water at roots. Cut potato haulms at first sign and lift after two weeks. Check BlightSpy forecasts.
  • Clubroot: Prevention is key. Lime if acid, improve drainage, resistant varieties, 4+ years between brassicas, clean tools.
  • Pigeons: Net brassicas properly with taut netting.

Produce, Plants And Seeds

  • Produce, plants and seeds sharing via the members area – surplus plants, produce and seeds at monthly meetings and village coffee mornings. Bring surplus seedlings, cuttings, seeds and divisions. Label clearly with variety and any local notes. Only healthy plants. Open-pollinated or heirloom seed preferred. Take a little, leave some for others. Share growing notes. No diseased or mouldy material. Contact enquiries@ruddingtongardeners.co.uk to get involved.

Seed Sharing Etiquette

  • - Label clearly (variety, year, notes such as “does well on clay”)
  • - Only viable recent seed stored cool and dry
  • - Prefer open-pollinated / heirloom (label hybrids)
  • - Take a little, leave some
  • - Share local performance notes
  • - No diseased or mouldy seed
  • - Be generous and patient
