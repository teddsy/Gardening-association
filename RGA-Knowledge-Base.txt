ABOUT THE RGA
The Ruddington Gardeners’ Association (RGA) was founded in 1934. Open to everyone in Ruddington and beyond, of all ages and abilities. Membership £5 per year. Meetings: last Wednesday of most months, 7.30pm, The Hermitage, Wilford Lane, Ruddington. Contact: enquiries@ruddingtongardeners.co.uk

CLAY SOIL – DETAILED RULES
East Midlands clay is fertile but heavy, cold in spring, bakes and cracks in summer, holds winter water.
1. Never work it wet. If it sticks to boots and tools, leave it.
2. Feed from the top with bulky organic matter (well-rotted compost or manure). One barrowload per square metre makes a real difference.
3. Do not add sand to heavy clay – it can set like concrete.
4. Raised beds and no-dig improve drainage and stop trampling.
5. Mulch permanent plants (rhubarb, fruit, roses) to stop summer cracking.
Advantages: naturally fertile, holds water and nutrients well once structure is improved. Excellent for brassicas and beans.

NO-DIG METHOD – DETAILED
No routine digging or rotavating. Cover the ground each year with a thick layer of well-rotted organic matter (minimum 5 cm / 2 in, better 8–15 cm when starting). Worms and fungi do the work.
Starting a new bed: remove perennial weeds first, then overlapping cardboard (turf can stay under it), then 10–15 cm of compost. Plant straight in.
Yearly maintenance: lighter top-up mulch (5 cm is about 50 litres per square metre).
Paths: woodchip or cardboard-plus-woodchip keeps boots clean. Keep beds about 4 ft (1.2 m) wide so you can reach the middle without treading.
Lasagna beds work on the same principle but watch for slug hotels on the sides.

CLIMATE, FROST AND TIMING (NOTTINGHAM / RUDDINGTON AREA)
Last spring air frost: typically mid-April (city) to late April / early May in outlying or low-lying spots. Practical plant-out date for tender crops is end of May (RHS Midlands guidance).
First autumn air frost: typically early-to-mid November (can be mid-October some years).
Ground frost continues 2–4 weeks after the last air frost.
Hardy crops (broad beans, peas, brassicas, onions, potatoes): mid-April onwards, with fleece ready.
Tender crops (tomatoes, courgettes, beans, squash): late May in a normal year.
Always check the current forecast before planting out.

MONTHLY CALENDAR – DETAILED JOBS
January: Plan rotation and beds. Order seeds. Chit first-early potatoes late in the month. Clean tools.
February: Sow indoors (broad beans, peas, onions, leeks, lettuce, early brassicas). Top up no-dig beds. Do not walk on wet clay.
March: Sow outdoors under cover (carrots, parsnips, peas, spinach, radish). Plant first-early potatoes late March. Sow tomatoes and peppers indoors.
April: Main outdoor sowing month. Plant second-early potatoes. Harden off tender seedlings but do not plant out yet. Fleece ready for late snaps.
May: Plant out tender crops late in the month. Sow successional salads and French beans. Net brassicas against pigeons. Hoe little and often.
June: Finish planting tender crops. First harvests (broad beans, early potatoes). Watch blackfly and blight risk.
July: Harvest potatoes, beans, garlic, early courgettes. Sow spring cabbage and swede. Water fruiting crops. Blight watch.
August: Main harvest. Sow overwintering salads and spring cabbage. Clear finished beds and sow green manures or mulch.
September: Plant out spring cabbage. Harvest maincrop potatoes as foliage dies – do not leave them in wet clay. Cure squash.
October: Plant garlic and overwintering onion sets. Sow overwintering broad beans (Aquadulce). Mulch no-dig beds. Lift and store potatoes.
November: First frosts. Last garlic plantings if ground workable. Mulch beds and rhubarb. Check stores.
December: Quiet month. Plan, order seeds, clean tools, check stores. Mulch parsnips.

CROP GUIDES
Potatoes: Chit Feb/Mar. Early varieties from mid-late March, maincrop April. Earth up or mulch. Lift as foliage dies rather than leaving in wet clay. Slug-resistant varieties: Charlotte, Kestrel, Estima, Pentland Dell.
Brassicas: Net against pigeons and cabbage whites. Lime only if soil test shows acid. Grow on in pots before planting. Clubroot-resistant options: Crispus F1 sprouts, Clapton F1 cauliflower, Kilaxy/Kilaton cabbages.
Tomatoes: Sow indoors March. Plant out after late May. Under cover is more reliable. Water at roots. Look for blight-resistant outdoor varieties.
Beans & Peas: Peas from March. Runner and French beans after mid-May. Pinch broad bean tips once pods set to reduce blackfly.
Onions, Garlic, Leeks: Garlic Oct–Nov. Onion sets March/April. Leeks sown spring, planted summer.
Courgettes: One or two plants usually enough. Plenty of compost and sun.
Salads: Succession sow from spring. Winter salads under cloches.

SOIL, NO-DIG AND ROTATION – RESEARCHED GENERAL GUIDANCE (paraphrased from RHS, Garden Organic, BBC Gardeners' World and the National Allotment Society; see sources.md)
Clay basics: Clay holds nutrients and water well but is sticky, slow to warm in spring and prone to waterlogging. Early sowings are the hard part; maincrop vegetables and tree fruit usually do very well, while raspberries and strawberries need more care with drainage.
Keep off wet clay: Walking or working it wet squashes the structure and takes a long time to recover. If you must step on a bed, stand on a plank to spread your weight. Beds about 4 ft (1.2 m) wide let you reach the middle from paths.
Warming the soil: Most seeds will not germinate below about 45°F (7°C). Cover beds with cloches or clear sheeting for a couple of weeks before early sowings, start seeds indoors, or simply sow a little later than the packet says.
Improvers: Bulky organic matter (compost, well-rotted manure, leafmould, composted bark) is the single best improver. Lime only acid clay (test first). Gypsum helps some clays – try a small patch. Sand or grit is far less effective than organic matter.
No-dig on clay: No-dig and raised beds suit clay particularly well. New bed: cut weeds down, cover with overlapping cardboard, then a thick mulch – Garden Organic suggests 6–8 in (15–20 cm) for clearing weedy ground; tough perennials such as bindweed and dock can take six months to a year to weaken. Established beds: top up yearly and sow or plant straight into the surface. Avoid old carpet – dyes and chemicals can leach into soil.
Permanent plants: Plant trees and bushes on a slight mound, around 10 in (25 cm), so roots don't sit in winter water. Mulch around fruit, rhubarb and asparagus to stop summer cracking.
Winter cover: Don't leave clay bare over winter – mulch it or sow a green manure. Choose the family carefully: mustard is a brassica (avoid where clubroot is a worry), field beans, clover and winter tares are legumes, grazing rye fits anywhere.
Crop rotation: Group crops by family and move them each year to reduce soil-borne problems like clubroot and onion white rot. Brassicas: cabbage, sprouts, cauliflower, kale, kohl rabi, oriental greens, radish, swede, turnip. Legumes: peas and all beans. Onions: onion, garlic, shallot, leek. Potato family: potato, tomato. Roots: beetroot, carrot, parsnip, celery, celeriac, fennel, parsley. Courgettes, squash, cucumbers, lettuce and sweetcorn can go wherever there is room, just not in the same spot every year. Keep asparagus, rhubarb and fruit bushes in their own permanent area. A simple plan: brassicas follow legumes and roots, potatoes follow brassicas. Keep notes of what went where.
Good starter crops (National Allotment Society suggestions): beetroot, broad beans, chard, climbing French beans, leeks, cut-and-come-again lettuce, onion sets in March, oriental leaves sown Aug–Sep, radish and perpetual spinach.
Nottingham frost figures: Met Office-based data puts the typical last air frost for NG postcodes around mid-April (about 16 April), and frost is usually past by early May (about 6 May) in nine years out of ten. First autumn frost is typically early-to-mid November. Late frosts do happen – the Midlands had air frost in early May 2021 – so the RGA rule of thumb stays: tender crops out at the end of May, after checking the forecast. Nottingham (Watnall) averages roughly 44°F (7°C) highs in January and 71°F (22°C) in July.

POTATO (veg)
Sow: Chit first earlies from Feb; no sowing (tubers)
Plant out: First earlies late Mar–early Apr; second earlies mid Apr; maincrop late Apr. about 6 in (15 cm) deep (or on the surface under compost for no-dig), earlies 12 in (30 cm) apart in rows 24 in (60 cm); maincrop 15 in (38 cm) x 30 in (75 cm)
Harvest: Earlies Jun–Jul; second earlies Jul–Aug; maincrop Aug–Sep
Clay & no-dig: No-dig: lay tubers on the surface and cover with 4–6 in (10–15 cm) compost, top up as shoots appear instead of earthing up. Lift maincrop promptly as foliage dies – tubers left in wet clay suffer slug damage.
Pests & problems: Slugs (keeled slugs worst on clay), blight (Jul–Sep), scab on dry/limed soil, wireworm in first year after grass
Good UK varieties (general): Charlotte; Kestrel; Estima; Pentland Dell; Sarpo Mira (blight resistant); Rocket (first early)
Active months: Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep

TOMATO (veg)
Sow: Indoors Feb–Mar (greenhouse) or late Mar–early Apr (outdoor types), 64–70°F (18–21°C)
Plant out: Greenhouse late Apr–May; outdoors early June after hardening off (end May at earliest)
Harvest: Jul–Oct
Clay & no-dig: Plant into compost-rich beds or large pots; clay holds water well but warms slowly – outdoor plants do best in the warmest, sheltered spot. Water at the base, little and often in pots, deeply in beds.
Pests & problems: Blight outdoors (warm wet spells, watch BlightSpy), blossom end rot (irregular watering), whitefly under glass, splitting
Good UK varieties (general): Crimson Crush (blight resistant); Shirley; Sungold; Gardener's Delight; Moneymaker; Ferline
In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): San Marzano Tomato Seeds, Tomato Ailsa Craig Seeds, Tomato Akoya F1, Tomato Alicante Seeds, Tomato Apero F1, Tomato Bauna F1, Tomato Bellandine F1 Seeds, Tomato Big Daddy F1 Seeds
Active months: Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct

CARROT (veg)
Sow: Outdoors Mar (under cover) to Jul, thinly, ½ in (1 cm) deep, rows 6 in (15 cm) apart
Plant out: Direct sown only – do not transplant
Harvest: Jun–Nov; maincrop can be stored
Clay & no-dig: Heavy clay causes forked roots: grow in a no-dig bed topped with 2–3 in (5–8 cm) fine compost, or choose short/round types. Avoid fresh manure.
Pests & problems: Carrot fly (fleece or 24 in/60 cm barrier, sow thinly, thin in evening), slugs on seedlings
Good UK varieties (general): Autumn King; Nantes 2; Flyaway F1 (carrot fly resistant); Resistafly F1; Chantenay Red Cored; Paris Market (round; good on clay)
In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): Carrot Adelaide F1 (RHS Award of Garden Merit), Carrot Amsterdam Forcing 3 Seeds, Carrot Attilio F1, Carrot Autumn King - Growers Pack (Award of Garden Merit), Carrot Autumn King Seeds (Award of Garden Merit), Carrot Berlicum Seeds, Carrot Chantenay Red Cored Seeds, Carrot Chantenay Royal Seeds
Active months: Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov

ONION (veg)
Sow: Seed indoors Jan–Feb; sets planted Mar–Apr; overwintering sets Sep–Oct
Plant out: Sets 4 in (10 cm) apart, rows 12 in (30 cm), tips just showing
Harvest: Summer onions Jul–Aug; overwintered Jun
Clay & no-dig: Shallow-rooted – good in no-dig compost. Avoid waterlogging over winter on clay; use raised beds for autumn sets.
Pests & problems: Onion white rot (long-lived, rotate), downy mildew in wet summers, onion fly, birds pulling sets
Good UK varieties (general): Sturon; Centurion F1; Red Baron; Radar (overwintering); Senshyu (overwintering)

GARLIC (veg)
Sow: Plant cloves Oct–Nov (best) or Feb–Mar for spring types
Plant out: Cloves 1 in (2.5 cm) below surface, 6 in (15 cm) apart, rows 12 in (30 cm)
Harvest: Jun–Jul when lower leaves yellow
Clay & no-dig: Needs a cold spell to split into cloves – autumn planting suits the Midlands. On wet clay plant into a raised/no-dig bed or start in modules and plant out in spring.
Pests & problems: Rust (orange spots, worse in damp years), white rot, birds lifting cloves
Good UK varieties (general): Solent Wight; Germidour; Elephant garlic (mild leek relative); Lautrec Wight
In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): Garlic Mustuard (Alliaria petiolata), Garlic Wild Ramsons (Allium ursinum) HP-V
Active months: Feb, Mar, Jun, Jul, Oct, Nov

LEEK (veg)
Sow: Indoors or seedbed Feb–Apr
Plant out: Jun–Jul when pencil thick; dib 6 in (15 cm) holes, drop in, water in (no backfill); 6 in (15 cm) apart
Harvest: Sep–Apr (very hardy, stands in ground)
Clay & no-dig: Thrives on fertile clay. No-dig: dibber straight through mulch. Lifting in winter is easier from a mulched bed than bare clay.
Pests & problems: Leek rust, allium leaf miner (cover with mesh Mar–Apr and Oct–Nov), leek moth
Good UK varieties (general): Musselburgh; Atlanta; Toledo; Bandit; Lyon 2
In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): Leek Autora F1, Leek Batter F1, Leek Below Zero F1, Leek Blue Solaise, Leek Giant Winter, Leek Jolant Seeds, Leek Lyon Seeds, Leek Mammoth Blanch (RHS Award of Garden Merit)

CABBAGE (veg)
Sow: Spring Apr–May (summer/autumn types); Jul–Aug (spring cabbage); in modules
Plant out: 4–6 weeks after sowing; 12–18 in (30–45 cm) apart, firm in well
Harvest: Year-round depending on type
Clay & no-dig: Brassicas love firm, fertile clay – do not loosen. Lime only if a soil test shows acid (helps clubroot).
Pests & problems: Pigeons, cabbage white caterpillars, cabbage root fly (collars), clubroot, slugs

CAULIFLOWER (veg)
Sow: Indoors/modules Mar–May for summer/autumn types
Plant out: May–Jul, 24 in (60 cm) apart, never let dry out
Harvest: Jun–Nov (overwinter types spring)
Clay & no-dig: Needs rich, moisture-retentive soil – clay is good once structured. Any check (drought, being potbound) causes small 'buttons'.
Pests & problems: As other brassicas; also heads yellowing in sun (fold leaves over)

BROAD BEAN (veg)
Sow: Oct–Nov (Aquadulce) or Feb–Apr; 2 in (5 cm) deep

PARSNIP (veg)
Sow: Direct Mar–May (soil above ~46°F / 8°C); use fresh seed each year
Plant out: Direct only, thin to 6 in (15 cm)
Harvest: Oct–Mar (sweeter after frost)
Clay & no-dig: Slow, erratic germination in cold clay – wait until April, sow into a fine compost tilth. Long roots may fork in clay; shorter varieties help.

In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): Salsify Sandwich Island, Scorzonera Long Black Maxima
Active months: Apr, May, Oct, Nov, Dec

MANGETOUT & SUGAR SNAP (veg)
Sow: Mar–Jun outdoors (or modules Feb under cover); successional sowings
Plant out: 2–3 in (5–8 cm) apart in double rows; support with netting or pea sticks 3–5 ft (1–1.5 m)
Harvest: May–Aug; pick flat pods (mangetout) or plump snaps every day or two
Clay & no-dig: Like peas: clay is fine once warmed. Start early batches in modules so wet clay doesn't rot seed. Net against pigeons and mice. Pick young – over-mature pods go tough.
Pests & problems: Mice, pigeons, pea moth (mesh helps), powdery mildew late season
Good UK varieties (general): Oregon Sugar Pod; Carouby de Maussane; Delikett (sugar snap); Sugar Ann; Shiraz (purple)
In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): Pea Carouby de Maussane (Mangetout), Pea Oregon Sugar Pod - Mangetout (RHS Award of Garden Merit)
Active months: Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug

PLUM & DAMSON (fruit)
Sow: Plant bare-root Nov–Mar; container trees any mild spell
Plant out: Warm sheltered site; avoid frost pockets. Fan-train on a south/west wall if space or frost is a worry
Harvest: Jul–Oct depending on variety (Victoria mid-season; damsons later)
Clay & no-dig: Fertile clay suits plums once drainage is decent – plant on a slight mound and mulch, don't dig a sump. Blossom is early and frost-tender; fleece on cold nights. Check allotment rules before planting trees. Victoria is partly self-fertile; many others need a pollination partner.
Pests & problems: Plum moth, aphids, silver leaf (prune Apr–Jul only), brown rot, bullfinches on buds
Good UK varieties (general): Victoria; Opal; Czar; Marjorie's Seedling; Shropshire Prune (damson)
Active months: Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov

CHERRY (fruit)
Sow: Plant bare-root Nov–Mar
Plant out: Sweet cherries: sunny south/west wall or open sunny site. Acid (Morello) cherries tolerate north walls. Well-drained fertile soil
Harvest: Jun–Aug; net against birds before colouring
Clay & no-dig: Needs free drainage – plant on a mound in clay and keep the graft union above soil. Fan-trained dwarfing rootstocks suit allotments better than large standards. Check site rules. Netting is essential or birds take the lot.
Pests & problems: Birds, cherry blackfly, bacterial canker, silver leaf, splitting after rain
Good UK varieties (general): Stella (self-fertile sweet); Sunburst; Morello (acid); Celeste; Penny
Active months: Jan, Feb, Mar, Jun, Jul, Aug, Nov

FIG (fruit)
Sow: Plant container or root-restricted trees in spring
Plant out: South/south-west wall; restrict roots in a 2 ft (60 cm) slab-lined pit or large pot for better fruiting
Harvest: Aug–Oct (usually one reliable UK crop of embryonic overwintered figs)
Clay & no-dig: Wants heat and drainage – a sunny wall and restricted roots matter more than rich clay. Mulch; protect embryonic fruits and tips with fleece in hard frosts. Pot-grown trees can move to a shed/greenhouse for winter in cold spots.
Pests & problems: Birds, red spider mite under glass, winter cold killing embryo fruits, coral spot on dead wood
Good UK varieties (general): Brown Turkey; Brunswick; White Marseille; Ice Crystal
Active months: Mar, Apr, May, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov

BLACKBERRY & HYBRID BERRIES (fruit)
Sow: Plant bare-root Nov–Mar; pot plants autumn or spring
Plant out: Along wires 5–6 ft (1.5–1.8 m) tall; 6–8 ft (1.8–2.4 m) between plants for trailing types, less for upright thornless
Harvest: Jul–Sep; hybrids (tayberry, loganberry, boysenberry) similar timing
Clay & no-dig: More forgiving than raspberries on clay if you mulch and avoid waterlogging. Train on wires; tip-rooting canes can escape – contain them. Thornless cultivars are kinder in small plots. Cut out fruited canes after harvest.
Pests & problems: Birds, raspberry beetle, spur blight, cane spot; thorny types hard to handle
Good UK varieties (general): Loch Ness; Loch Tay; Karaka Black; Oregon Thornless; Tayberry Buckingham
In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): Blackberry Karaka Black - 2L Potted Bush - MARCH DELIVERY, Blackberry Loch Ness - 2 Litre Potted Bush - MARCH DELIVERY, Blackberry Loch Tay - 2 Litre Potted Bush - MARCH DELIVERY, Loganberries - 2 Litre pot - MARCH 2027 DELIVERY
Active months: Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul, Aug, Sep, Nov

GRAPEVINE (fruit)
Sow: Plant dormant vines Nov–Mar
Plant out: Outdoor wine types on a warm south wall/fence; dessert grapes usually need a greenhouse in the Midlands
Harvest: Sep–Oct when berries taste fully sweet (they don't ripen off the vine)
Clay & no-dig: Needs sun and free drainage – mound-plant and mulch on clay. Outdoor cropping is realistic for early wine varieties; dessert grapes under glass. Prune in winter (and summer laterals) or vines run wild. Check allotment rules for permanent climbers.
Pests & problems: Powdery mildew, wasps on ripe bunches, birds, under-ripe fruit in cool summers
Good UK varieties (general): Regent; Rondo; Phoenix; Boskoop Glory (outdoor dessert); Black Hamburg (glass)
Active months: Jan, Feb, Mar, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov

WHITECURRANT (fruit)
Sow: Plant bare-root Nov–Mar
Plant out: 5 ft (1.5 m) apart; sun or light shade; bush or cordon
Harvest: Jul; pick whole strigs when translucent
Clay & no-dig: Same family as redcurrant – fertile clay is fine with mulch and a slight mound against winter wet. Net against birds. Prune to an open goblet; fruit on old wood spurs.
Pests & problems: Birds, aphids, gooseberry sawfly (also attacks currants), leaf spot
Good UK varieties (general): White Versailles; Blanka; White Grape
Active months: Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul, Nov

OREGANO & MARJORAM (herb)
Sow: Indoors Mar–Apr or buy plants; Greek oregano is the classic pizza flavour
Plant out: Late May, 8–12 in (20–30 cm) apart in sun and sharp drainage
Harvest: May–Sep; pick before flowering for best flavour; dry sprigs for winter
Clay & no-dig: Hates wet clay around the roots – raised bed, grit-mixed compost or a pot. Mediterranean herbs want sun and dryness more than fertility. Cut back after flowering to keep bushy.
Pests & problems: Root rot on wet soil, aphids; little else
Good UK varieties (general): Greek oregano; Compact oregano; Sweet marjoram (annual/tender); Golden oregano
In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): Herb Sweet Marjoram - Perennial, Organic Marjoram, Pot (Origanum vulgare), Organic Marojoram Sweet (Origanum marjorana), Herb Oregano Greek - Perennial
Active months: Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep

BAY (herb)
Sow: Buy plants (seed is slow); plant spring so they settle before winter
Plant out: Sheltered sunny or light-shade spot, or a pot (easier to protect). Avoid wet ground and cold winds
Harvest: Year-round; dry or freeze leaves
Clay & no-dig: Evergreen Mediterranean shrub – on Midlands clay grow in a large pot with free-draining compost and move to a sheltered wall or cold greenhouse in harsh winters. Clip to shape; leaves flavour stews and bouquet garni.
Pests & problems: Bay sucker, scale, leaf scorch from cold wind, root rot in wet clay
Good UK varieties (general): Laurus nobilis (sweet bay); Aurea (golden); Angustifolia (willow-leaf)
Active months: Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec

TARRAGON (herb)
Sow: French tarragon: buy plants (seed is usually Russian). Russian from seed in spring
Plant out: Late spring in sun and free drainage; French best in a pot you can shelter in winter
Harvest: May–Sep; tip-prune often; freeze or dry (milder when dried)
Clay & no-dig: French tarragon (best flavour) needs dryish roots and winter protection – pot culture on clay. Russian is hardier but coarser. Don't confuse with invasive Russian allowed to seed everywhere.
Pests & problems: Winter wet killing French plants; little pest pressure
Good UK varieties (general): French tarragon; Russian tarragon
In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): Herb Russian Tarragon - Perennial
Active months: Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep

LEMON BALM (herb)
Sow: Spring outdoors or modules; or divide established clumps
Plant out: 12–18 in (30–45 cm) apart; sun or light shade
Harvest: Apr–Oct; fresh leaves for tea; cut hard if it flops
Clay & no-dig: Easy perennial that tolerates clay. Can self-seed freely – deadhead if you don't want a patch. Good for bees. Pot if you want to limit spread.
Pests & problems: Few; mainly seediness and mint-like spread
Good UK varieties (general): Common lemon balm; All Gold; Compacta
In the Kings Seeds range (members' group order): Herb Balm Orange 'Mandarina' (Melissa), Herb Lemon Balm (Melissa) Perennial
Active months: Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep

PESTS AND PROBLEMS (ORGANIC-FIRST)
Slugs: Worst on clay and wet autumns. Torchlight picking, traps, encourage predators. Lift potatoes promptly. Slug-resistant potato varieties help. Nematodes work less well on poorly drained clay.
Aphids / Blackfly: Squash colonies. Pinch broad bean tips. Encourage ladybirds.
Potato & Tomato Blight: Warm wet weather. No sprays for UK gardeners. Wider spacing, water at roots. Cut potato haulms at first sign and lift after two weeks. Check BlightSpy forecasts.
Clubroot: Prevention is key. Lime if acid, improve drainage, resistant varieties, 4+ years between brassicas, clean tools.
Pigeons: Net brassicas properly with taut netting.
